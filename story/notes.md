# Notes — decisions log & rejected directions

Append-only working notes. Every phase that establishes new canon (names, places, world rules, events) or rejects a creative direction records it here with a short issue/date reference. The files under `story/` are the canon; this file is the running commentary on how it got that way.

## Decisions

### Issue #1 — Idea phase (2026-05-12)

- **Infrastructure type:** Chose civic/municipal infrastructure (electricity, heating, water, transit) over digital/computational or military. Municipal infrastructure makes the complicity theme universal — every citizen benefits, every citizen is implicated.
- **City name:** Ashmark. Evokes ash (combustion residue) and mark (a brand, a record). Considered "Cinderwell" and "Pyrevault" — both too on-the-nose.
- **Technology name:** Thanalic engineering (from Greek *thanatos*, death). Preferred over "necrotic energy" (too horror-coded) and "animic conversion" (too clinical).
- **Protagonist:** Lena Morrow, a furnace technician ("stoker"). Chose a mid-level worker over a scientist/executive/outsider — keeps the complicity theme grounded. She is not a rebel; she is forced into action by personal loss.
- **Supporting cast sketched:** Edric Calloway (Registrar clerk, reluctant ally) and Superintendent Voss (antagonist with personal stake in the fraud). Full character development deferred to World Bible — Characters phase.
- **Central mechanism:** "Quiet release" exemptions are a fraud — all souls are harvested regardless. This makes the betrayal both personal (Lena's mother) and systemic (every citizen who paid for an exemption was scammed).
- **Length:** Novelette (~12,000–18,000 words), not a novel. The cast is small and the timeline compressed; more length would dilute the claustrophobic workplace atmosphere.
- **Style:** Literary industrial fantasy with noir undertones. Close third POV on Lena, past tense, sombre tone with dry humour. No info-dumps; world-building through workplace detail. See `## Style` in `AGENTS.md` / `CLAUDE.md`.

## Rejected directions

### Issue #1 — Idea phase (2026-05-12)

- **Digital/computational infrastructure:** Souls powering an internet analogue or AI. Rejected — too abstract, harder to ground in physical detail, and the workplace-mundanity angle works better with tangible infrastructure (pipes, furnaces, gauges).
- **Military application:** Souls as weapons or powering war machines. Rejected — shifts the genre toward military SF/fantasy and away from the labour/complicity theme.
- **Protagonist as outsider/journalist:** Someone discovering the system for the first time. Rejected — the story is stronger when the protagonist has always known and participated; discovery of personal betrayal is the catalyst, not discovery of the system itself.
- **First-person POV:** Considered for intimacy but rejected — close third gives the same interiority while allowing the narrative to describe things Lena wouldn't articulate to herself, especially the gap between what she knows and what she admits.
- **Present tense:** Rejected — past tense suits the reflective, noir-adjacent tone better and avoids the breathless quality that present tense can impose on slower, atmospheric scenes.
