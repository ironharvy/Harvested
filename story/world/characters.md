# World Bible — Characters

## Major Characters

### Maren Voss

**Role:** Conduit technician, Harvest Station Nine.

**Age:** 36.

**Physical description:** Medium height, lean from years of climbing cell-rack ladders. Dark hair kept short and practical — she cuts it herself. Pale complexion common to Ashward's overcast climate. Calloused hands, blunt nails. A faded burn scar on her left forearm from a containment seal that failed in her second year. She dresses in the Bureau-issued grey coveralls at work and wears them home more often than she'd admit. Her face is composed, even blank, in a way that reads as competence to colleagues and as absence to anyone who looks longer.

**Relationships:**
- **Irina Voss** (mother, deceased): The relationship was strained and largely silent for the last several years — Maren's work at the station sat between them like something neither could name. Irina never said she disapproved. She didn't need to.
- **Director Cassan Leth** (supervisor): Professional, respectful, and one-sided in its warmth. Leth considers Maren his best technician. Maren considers Leth her boss and does not think about it further — until she must.
- **Jorin Dael** (colleague): The closest thing Maren has to a friend at the station. They eat lunch together, occasionally share a drink after shift. He is the only person she considers trusting, and even then she hesitates.

**Aspirations:** Before the story, none that she would articulate. Maren's ambition curdled into routine years ago. She wanted, once, to transfer to a newer station in a better city — cleaner equipment, lower caseloads, the quiet implication of less guilt. She stopped wanting that. What she wants now — to save her mother's soul — is the first desire she has felt in years, and it frightens her.

**Flaws:** Maren's defining flaw is her cultivated numbness. Twelve years of compartmentalisation have made her efficient and hollow. She is not cruel, but she has been complicit, and the distinction matters less than she thinks. She is also isolating — she does not ask for help easily, does not confide, and tends to act alone even when alliance would serve her. Her moral awakening is genuine but dangerously narrow: she cares about her mother's soul because it is her mother's, and the story's deeper challenge is whether that care can generalise.

### Director Cassan Leth

**Role:** Station Director, Harvest Station Nine.

**Age:** 54.

**Physical description:** Tall, solidly built, with a broad face that defaults to an expression of patient concern. Greying hair combed back neatly. He wears the Bureau director's uniform — charcoal suit, silver pin at the lapel — with the unselfconscious ease of a man who has worn a uniform of some kind his entire adult life. His hands are soft; he hasn't touched a containment cell in twenty years. His office smells of weak tea and filing-cabinet dust.

**Relationships:**
- **Maren Voss** (subordinate): He genuinely values her. In a system that churns through staff, her steadiness is rare. He sees in her a version of the commitment he prizes — quiet, competent, uncomplaining. He does not understand that her quietness is not agreement.
- **The Bureau** (institution): Leth is a true institutional man. His loyalty is not cynical; he believes the harvest is a moral good, that the dead owe the living, that the system serves the common welfare. He is the kind of person who makes monstrous systems functional: not by being monstrous himself but by being sincere.
- **His family** (off-stage): A wife and two grown children. He mentions them occasionally in the mild, affectionate way of a man who considers his domestic life unremarkable and his professional life his real identity.

**Aspirations:** Leth wants Harvest Station Nine to run well. He wants to be the kind of director who is remembered as fair, steady, and effective. He has ambitions for a Regional Directorate appointment someday, but they are patient ambitions — he is a man who believes in earning things through time served.

**Flaws:** His sincerity is his flaw. Leth cannot see the system as monstrous because he has built his identity around its necessity. When Maren's actions threaten the station's order, his response is not villainous — it is wounded. He feels betrayed. He responds with the tools he knows: procedure, authority, the calm invocation of rules. He is incapable of imagining that the rules themselves are the problem.

### Irina Voss

**Role:** Maren's mother. Deceased before the story's present action; present only as a soul in a containment cell and in Maren's memories.

**Age at death:** 61.

**Physical description (in life, as Maren remembers her):** A small woman who grew smaller. Grey-blonde hair she wore pinned up. Hands that were always busy — mending, sorting, wiping surfaces. A face that held worry the way a shelf holds dust: in the creases, in the corners, never fully cleared. She dressed neatly, modestly, in clothes that lasted.

**Physical description (as a soul in containment):** Not visible to the naked eye under normal suppression. When the suppression field weakens, she manifests as a faint luminescence inside the glass — a cold blue-white light that pulses unevenly, like breath. In the final moment, when Maren removes the suppression sigils and releases the cell, Irina coalesces briefly: a shape that is almost a woman, a light that is almost a face, recognition that is almost a word.

**Relationships:**
- **Maren Voss** (daughter): They loved each other in the inarticulate way of people who never learned to say it directly. Irina worried about Maren's work — not because she understood what it entailed, but because she sensed what it was doing to her daughter. She never asked Maren to quit. She never asked Maren anything that might require an answer neither of them could bear.
- **The system:** Irina was an ordinary citizen of Ashward. She accepted the harvest the way she accepted weather. Her own death and siphoning were unremarkable to the Bureau — one more soul, properly processed, except that her deferral petition — filed within the window — was never processed.

**Aspirations:** In life, small ones — that Maren would visit more, that the damp in the kitchen wall would stop spreading, that things would be all right. In death, none that the Bureau recognises.

**Flaws:** Irina's passivity — her acceptance of the world as it was — is the quality Maren inherited and the quality the story asks Maren to break from. Irina never questioned the system. She never had reason to. That is not a moral failing, but it is the soil in which Maren's complicity grew.

## Minor Characters

### Jorin Dael

**Role:** Conduit technician, Harvest Station Nine. Same rank as Maren, roughly the same tenure.

**Age:** 39.

**Physical description:** Stocky, dark-skinned, with a neatly kept beard going grey at the chin. Laugh lines around his eyes that deepen when he's anxious — which, lately, is often. He moves through the station with the unhurried ease of someone who knows every corridor.

**Relationships:** Maren's closest colleague. They share the easy camaraderie of people who work the same grim job and cope with the same dark humour. Jorin is married, has a young daughter, and this is the fact that defines his limits — he will help Maren up to the point where helping her threatens what he has at home.

**Aspirations:** To keep his head down, keep his family fed, and retire with his pension. He is not a coward, but he is a realist, and in this world the two look identical.

**Flaws:** His decency is bounded. He knows, in the vague way that most station workers know, that the system is ugly. He does not pretend otherwise. But he will not act on that knowledge if acting costs him anything real.

### Poul Hadren

**Role:** Intake clerk, Harvest Station Nine.

**Age:** 27.

**Physical description:** Thin, bespectacled, perpetually cold — he wears a cardigan over his Bureau shirt even in summer. Ink stains on his fingers.

**Relationships:** A junior bureaucrat who processes intake forms and manages the station's portion of the soul registry. He interacts with Maren in the course of routine paperwork. He is neither an ally nor an obstacle — he is the system's paperwork given a face.

**Significance:** Poul is the person who processed (or failed to process) Irina Voss's deferral petition. Whether this was deliberate neglect, clerical error, or quiet instruction from above is something Maren may never fully learn — and the ambiguity matters more than the answer.

---

### Supervisor Gren

**Role:** Night-shift supervisor, Harvest Station Nine.

**Age:** 48.

**Physical description:** A heavyset woman with cropped grey hair and a voice that carries through concrete walls. She has the weary authority of someone who has managed difficult people for too long.

**Significance:** Gren runs the overnight shift when Maren will need to access her mother's cell. She is not a villain — she is a schedule, a set of keys, and a pair of eyes that Maren must account for. An obstacle of logistics, not malice.

---

### The Field Teams

Not individual characters but a presence in the world. Bureau of Reclamation field teams — the people who perform the draw at the moment of death — are mentioned but not seen in the story. They are the system's hands. Maren has never been one; station technicians and field agents occupy different branches of the Bureau, with different training and different coping mechanisms. Field agents are rumoured to burn out faster.
